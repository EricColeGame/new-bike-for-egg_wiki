### A Simple Way to Test Any New Bike

1. Pick one zone route and ride it three times on your current bike. Write down your average time.
2. Unlock or rent the new bike and repeat the exact same route three times.
3. Compare averages, not single runs — one clean run proves nothing.
4. Note handling differences on corners, since a faster bike that wipes out more often can be a net loss.
5. Record the whole test. Even a failed comparison makes solid footage.

If the new bike wins by a wide margin on the same route, it is worth the grind. If it wins by a second or two, the multiplier is probably close and your cash is better spent elsewhere.

## Community Reports vs. Patch Notes: What to Trust

The Bike For Egg community is active and generous with information, but it also repeats rumors until they sound like facts. A quick reliability check saves you from grinding toward a bike that does not exist or does not perform the way a comment claimed.

| Claim you will see | Where it usually comes from | How much to trust it |
|---|---|---|
| "This bike is the fastest in the game" | Clip titles and comment sections | Low until you time it yourself |
| "The new bike costs X eggs" | Shop UI and patch notes | High if you saw it in-game |
| "There is a secret bike in a hidden zone" | Community reports and Discord chatter | Mixed — fun to test, don't build a video on it |
| "The multiplier is X" | Fan ranking posts and wikis | Medium — check the publish date first |
| "They nerfed the old bike" | Player experience after an update | Medium — confirm with a timed run |

The pattern is simple: anything you can see in the game client outranks anything you read. Community reports are best used as a list of things to test, not as conclusions.
