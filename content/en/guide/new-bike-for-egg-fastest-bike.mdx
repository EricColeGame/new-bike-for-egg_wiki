## Why the New Bike for Egg Rewrote the Record Books

Eighty-nine miles per hour on leg power alone sounds like a typo until you see the shell. The new bike for egg-shaped speed runs is the clearest proof that aerodynamics, not raw wattage, decides how fast a pedal machine can travel. If you want to understand what separates the fastest bike in the world from everything else on two wheels, the new bike for egg is the machine to study.

The record came from Aerovelo, a Canadian engineering team that treats human-powered vehicles as a design problem rather than a weekend hobby. Their bike, Eta — named after the engineering symbol for efficiency — reached 89.59 mph, adding nearly 3 mph to the team's own previous mark. [Popular Mechanics' coverage of the egg-shaped record bike](https://www.popularmechanics.com/technology/a22946/human-powered-speed-record-aerovelo/) walks through the run, and the details are worth unpacking piece by piece.

### The Egg Is the Whole Trick

At record speeds, most of a rider's energy goes into pushing air aside. A shell that keeps airflow attached and frontal area tiny changes the math entirely. The pilot cannot see over the nose of the machine, so the cockpit stays sealed and vision comes from cameras feeding displays inside — a design choice that exists purely to protect the aerodynamic shape.

### Gearing Built for Exactly One Job

The 93-tooth chainring is the visual signature of the new bike for egg-style engineering. Paired with a second-stage 39:18 ratio, it lets a trained rider spin the rear wheel to speeds that would be unrideable with ordinary gearing. The tradeoff is acceleration: the machine takes a long time to build speed, so the rider must hit a precise sprint window. That is exactly what the onboard heads-up display is for.

## What Builders Can Steal From the New Bike for Egg

You will probably never ride a fully enclosed streamliner, but the design logic transfers to ordinary bikes and to anyone chasing personal speed goals.

- **Fix fit before buying parts.** The rider's position inside the shell was optimized for leverage first, visibility second. On a normal bike, a professional fit delivers more free speed than most component upgrades.
- **Attack frontal area early.** Narrower bars, a lower stem, tighter clothing, and a tucked helmet all reduce the hole you punch in the air.
- **Keep the power path stiff.** The carbon-fiber frame exists to stop flex from eating watts. Stiff shoes and a well-supported pedaling platform do the same job on a road bike.
- **Match gearing to the course.** A 93-tooth ring makes sense on a flat salt strip and nowhere else. Choose chainrings for the terrain you actually ride.
- **Rehearse the effort.** The pilot knew the exact second to sprint. Practice your own decisive efforts — a climb, a town-line sprint, a Strava segment — so the timing is automatic.
- **Never trade control for speed.** Cameras and a sealed cockpit were a deliberate safety decision, not a gimmick.

| Lesson from the record machine | Everyday version |
| --- | --- |
| Frontal area beats brute force | Tuck lower, narrow the bars, smooth the clothing |
| A stiff frame transfers power | Prioritize frame, shoe, and pedal stiffness |
| Gearing should match the course | Pick chainrings for your terrain, not for bragging rights |
| Vision and control come first | Never sacrifice braking or sightlines for speed |

## FAQ

### What exactly is the new bike for egg?

It is the nickname for the fully enclosed, egg-shaped human-powered streamliner built by Aerovelo. The shell houses the rider in a reclined position and is designed so that airflow stays smooth around the body at very high speed.

### How fast is the fastest bike in the world?

The Aerovelo Eta reached 89.59 mph on a solo run, beating the team's own previous record by almost 3 mph. Faster bicycle figures exist, but those runs used a pace car for shelter from the wind and are not directly comparable.

### Can you buy a bike like this?

No. The new bike for egg is a one-off record machine that needs a closed course, a support crew, and a highly trained pilot. Riders who want similar aerodynamics on public roads usually look at velomobiles, which offer a partial shell and far more practicality.

### Why does the rider lie down instead of sitting upright?

Two reasons. A reclined position lets the rider push against a seat back for more leverage, and it lowers the body enough to fit inside the small aerodynamic shell. Both advantages only matter because the shell itself removes so much drag.
